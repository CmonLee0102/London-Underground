# London-Underground
London Underground — Ride the Tube

Step onto the platform, pick a station, and ride the line — the way London does it.

London Underground is an interactive simulation of the Tube network, built in the authentic design language of Transport for London. From the Johnston-style roundel to the official line colours, every detail is crafted to feel like the real thing — and it all runs from a single, self-contained web page with no installation, accounts, or dependencies.

Explore a Beck-style schematic map that you can pan, zoom, and pinch, with a stylised city backdrop of streets, parks, and the River Thames beneath the lines. Filter by any of the twelve lines, or search for a station with instant autocomplete. Choose your destination and the map dives in, then hands you over to the ride itself.

The journey screen puts you inside the carriage: a split-flap departure board clatters through to your station's name, tunnel lights streak past the windows, the train sways gently on the rails, and the doors slide open on arrival to reveal the platform — all accompanied by the unmistakable two-tone chime and a British-voiced "Mind the gap" announcement.

Arrive, and each station opens into a rich detail page powered live by Wikipedia: a hero photograph, a concise description, fare zone, opening year and years in service, an "About" write-up, and a Lines & connections panel showing every line's termini and the adjacent stations — each one tappable to ride onward. Nearby landmarks, step-free accessibility information, and links out to Wikipedia and Google Maps round it out.
